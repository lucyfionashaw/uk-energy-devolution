Does party matter?

Does the council's party change what gets built?

Every local project is decided by a council, and councils have a political colour. Crediting each decision to the party that controlled the council on the day it was made, here is who approves what — by capacity, per unit of land, and how fast.

Capacity & speed

Who approves the most, and how fast

The Conservatives have approved the most capacity outright — they run the most councils and the most land. Labour-run councils are the fastest of the major parties to reach a decision.

Per unit of land

Approval per km² of land controlled

Raw capacity favours whoever governs the most countryside. Normalising by the land each party actually controlled — MW approved per km², per year — reorders it: the denser, more urban Labour and Lib Dem councils approve the most for the land they have, while the land-rich Conservative, SNP and independent-led shires approve the least per acre. (Green and Reform are left off here — too few decisions, and too recently in control, to give a stable rate.)

A big caveat: Scotland

The two lowest bars — SNP and Independent — are both dominated by Scotland. Independents' vast land base is largely rural Scottish councils (Highland alone is ~24,000 km²). Scotland consents its big renewables nationally, not locally: of 111 granted Scottish onshore wind farms of 50 MW or more, 109 were decided by Scottish Ministers, not councils. So this chart — which only sees council decisions — understates Scotland. Read the SNP and Independent bars as "what Scottish councils approve," not "what Scotland builds."

Approval rate

The rate they say yes

Approval rates are high under every party — roughly 81% to 93% of decided projects. Labour-run councils approve at a slightly higher rate than Conservative ones; independent-led councils are the lowest. At this altitude the gaps are modest.
