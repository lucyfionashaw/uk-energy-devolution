Does party matter?

Does the council's party change what gets built?

Every local project is decided by a council, and councils have a political colour. Crediting each decision to the party that controlled the council on the day it was made, here is who approves what — by capacity, per unit of land, and how fast.

Capacity & speed

Who approves the most, and how fast

The Conservatives have approved the most capacity outright — they run the most councils and the most land. Labour-run councils are the fastest of the major parties to reach a decision.

Raw capacity favours whoever governs the most countryside. A big caveat: Scotland

The two lowest bars — SNP and Independent — are both dominated by Scotland, and both are held down by the same thing. Independents' vast land base is largely rural Scottish councils (Highland alone is ~24,000 km²); the SNP has run much of Scotland for years. But Scotland consents its big renewables nationally, not locally: of 111 granted Scottish onshore wind farms of 50 MW or more, 109 were decided by Scottish Ministers, not councils. So this chart — which only sees council decisions — understates Scotland. Read the SNP and Independent bars as "what Scottish councils approve," not "what Scotland builds."

Approval rate

The rate they say yes

Approval rates are high under every party — roughly 81% to 93% of decided projects. Labour-run councils approve at a slightly higher rate than Conservative ones; independent-led councils are the lowest. At this altitude the gaps are modest.

Approvals by year

Who actually approved it, year by year

The Guardian reported that roughly twice as much renewable capacity was approved in Labour's first stretch in office as in the Conservatives' last. But Westminster doesn't grant applications — councils do. Crediting each year's approvals to the party controlling the deciding council shows where the jump really is: not a clean 2024 step-change, but a pipeline that swells into 2025, granted by councils of every colour (Conservative-run ones most of all).

The "2×" is real, but it's a period, not a cause

Matched 21-month windows do show it — about 30 GW of wind and solar approved after July 2024 against 16 GW before. But the pipeline was already surging under the Conservatives, the biggest single year is 2025, and by project count the later approvals are fewer but larger. Whoever sits in Westminster, the grants are made by councils — most of them Conservative-controlled.

Where national policy does bite: onshore wind

The one place Westminster policy bites is onshore wind. England's 2015 de-facto ban cut English onshore applications from ~60 a year to nearly zero for a decade; after the ban was lifted in 2024 they rebounded to 42 in 2025. Scotland, with no ban, kept approving 0.5–1.7 GW a year throughout. National government matters through policy — not by deciding the applications.

A note on Northern Ireland

NI councils sit outside these charts — they use STV power-sharing and a separate, shorter control series, so they aren't part of the GB party comparison.
