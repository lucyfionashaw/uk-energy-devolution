Section 03 · The Machine

How long does Britain take to say yes?

Approval is the easy part. The harder question is how long everything takes — to reach a decision, to break ground, to reach the grid — and how that changes by technology, by project size, and by who runs the council.

How often is "often"?

The median council sees one of these every three years

A planning committee only gets good at what it does repeatedly. So how often does a single authority actually face a major solar or battery scheme? Counting every authority in every year — including all the years they saw nothing — the answer is: rarely.

How often is "often"

The median authority received just 2 major (10 MW+) solar or battery applications across six years — one roughly every three years — and 28% received none at all. Even in 2025, the busiest year on record, 55% of authorities saw not a single one. The mean (4.8) is more than twice the median because the distribution is wildly skewed: North Yorkshire alone took 57.

Why this matters

Expertise is a function of repetition. An authority that meets a grid-scale battery once every three years has no standing capability to assess one — no in-house specialists, no precedent, no muscle memory — while the developer opposite has done fifty. That asymmetry, rather than hostility, may explain a good deal of the delay measured elsewhere on this page. The median is unchanged at 2 when repeat applications on the same site are merged.

The backlog

What's stuck in the queue — and for how long
