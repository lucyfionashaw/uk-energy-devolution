Section 04 · Technology

What Britain is trying to build

What developers propose has shifted repeatedly over fifteen years: the solar surge, the arrival of grid-scale batteries, and the sharp decline of onshore wind in England after 2015.

The shifting pipeline

What the system is asked to process, year by year

Two very different stories, depending on how you count. By applications, solar and batteries swamp everything — thousands of individually small schemes the planning system has to churn through. By capacity, a handful of giant offshore wind farms rival the entire solar fleet. Toggle between them: the gap is the workload problem in one picture.

Who applies

A privately-owned pipeline

Classifying every applicant by name: the pipeline is overwhelmingly commercial. The public sector — councils, schools, the NHS — applies fairly often but almost entirely for rooftop-scale solar on its own buildings. Community-owned energy is barely visible: 70 projects and 0.3 GW applied in 36 years, less than a thousandth of the pipeline's capacity.

Source: Renewable Energy Planning Database (DESNZ, Q1 2026), Operator/Applicant field classified by name; resubmitted duplicates merged. Assumptions: "trust" is split by what the trust is (NHS trusts → NHS; academy trusts → education; investment and family trusts → private; development trusts → community); commercially-owned firms with community-sounding names (e.g. Community Windpower Ltd, Solar Options for Schools Ltd) are counted as private. The applicant may be an agent for another owner, and ownership can change after consent — REPD's dedicated community-scheme field is unpopulated, so name-based classification is the only route.
The divergence

The two approval stories

Overall approval rates have drifted up to ~90%. That headline sits above onshore wind, whose approval rate fell through the early 2010s and then recovered later in the decade.

Onshore wind

Onshore wind approval fell from ~64% in 2008 to 40% by 2014, around the run-up to the 2015 English restrictions, and has since climbed back above 80%.

Outcomes by technology

Approval rates across the board
